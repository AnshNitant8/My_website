Case Studies 3 projects
Context
IRCTC's Tatkal booking window opens at 10 AM — seats sell out within minutes. The existing session management creates a critical failure point: any network hiccup, accidental tab close, or timeout mid-booking forces a full restart, by which time seats are gone.
User Problem
Users who experience an error-triggered logout during peak Tatkal booking lose all booking context — train selection, passenger details, seat preferences — and must re-enter a queue that has moved on, resulting in a failed booking despite legitimate intent.
Existing Journey
Search train → Select seat → Fill passenger → Session expires → Restart from scratch → Seats gone
Proposed Solution
Quick Resume Booking — a session-state recovery mechanism that persists train selection, passenger details, and seat preferences for a configurable window post-logout. P0 feature: one-tap re-entry into a preserved booking flow, skipping re-entry of all previously confirmed data.
Feature Prioritization
P0: Passenger data persistence across session loss, one-tap resume entry.
P1: Seat hold notification, estimated availability window.
P2: Cross-device resume, booking history quick-fill.
Success Metrics
Reduction in Tatkal booking abandonment rate. Increase in booking completion rate during peak windows. User satisfaction score for Tatkal flow. Session recovery usage rate (adoption).
Operational Problem
Before every corporate insurance quote, sales reps manually searched for company data — MCA registration details, GST records, employee strength, turnover figures. Each lookup took 20–40 minutes. Data was inconsistent across reps, leading to proposal quality variations and junk leads entering the pipeline.
Existing Workflow
Rep receives lead → Manual MCA/GST lookup → Manual data entry → Prepare proposal
Product Opportunity
The company registration and GST data was publicly available through structured APIs. The opportunity was to build an internal lookup tool that pre-fetches verified company data on demand, authenticated using GSTIN — eliminating manual work and acting as a lead-qualification gate.
Solution Built
An automated company profile retrieval tool — integrated with GST and MCA data APIs. Single GSTIN input returns verified company name, registration status, employee strength, and turnover data in seconds. GST authentication layer filters unqualified/inactive entities before they enter the sales pipeline.
95%
Manual processing cut
95% reduction in manual data-fetching time per lead. 40% reduction in junk leads entering the sales pipeline through GST-based authentication.
Business Problem
Group corporate insurance policy issuance required manual document handling, offline approvals, and multi-step coordination across Sales, Operations, and Compliance. The process was error-prone, slow, and created an audit trail that existed only on paper.
Existing Workflow
Sales submits paper form → Ops manual entry → Offline approvals → Manual policy doc → Physical dispatch
My Role
Led end-to-end product delivery — gathered requirements from Sales, Operations, Compliance, and Legal. Defined workflow states, digital approval logic, and document generation requirements. Managed Agile delivery with Engineering and QA in Jira. Owned UAT coordination and launch.
Solution Delivered
A fully digital Master Policy Portal — online form capture, structured data entry validation, digital approval workflow with role-based access, automated policy document generation, and digital delivery. All manual processing steps replaced.

I spend more time understanding why a problem exists than rushing to what the solution could be. That means user interviews, journey mapping, and sitting with business stakeholders to separate symptoms from root causes. At Care Health, this meant shadowing sales teams before defining a single product requirement.

A good PRD is a decision log, not a wishlist. I write BRDs and PRDs that give engineers clarity, give QA a test framework, and give stakeholders a shared language. Prioritization isn't a framework exercise — it's a conversation about what matters most given constraints.
